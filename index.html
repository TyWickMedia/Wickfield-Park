The Park

A country stay, minutes from town

Wickfield RV Park sits right at the edge of the Stillwater city limits on N Jardot Rd — far enough out that the evenings are genuinely quiet, close enough that campus, groceries and dinner are a short drive away.

We kept it small on purpose. Eleven sites means there's always room between rigs, the awning side stays open, and you're not listening to a neighbor's generator through the wall. It's a park built for people who want space — travelers passing through, contractors and travel nurses on a monthly stay, families in town for an Oklahoma State weekend, and snowbirds who'd rather not park in a lot.

Every site is a pull-through and every site is oversized, so a 45-foot coach with a toad pulls straight in, levels up, and stays hitched if you want it to.

And the utilities are on us. Water, sewer and 50 amp electric are included in every rate — nightly, weekly or monthly — so you can run both air conditioners through an Oklahoma August without watching a meter.

Questions

Good to know

The things guests ask us most. Anything else, just call or send a note.

When do you open?

Soon — we're finishing the park now and don't have an exact date to give yet, so we can't take reservations quite yet. Send us the dates you're thinking about and you'll be the first to hear when the booking calendar opens.

What does it cost to stay?

$60 a night, $250 a week, or $500 a month — and full hookups (water, sewer, 50 amp electric) plus WiFi are included in all three. The weekly rate works out to about $36 a night and the monthly to about $17. Nightly and weekly rates vary on Oklahoma State event weekends.

What's included in the rate?

Full hookups — water, sewer and 50 amp electric — plus park-wide WiFi, the dog park and the chipping green. That's nightly, weekly or monthly alike. No metered power, no utility add-ons, so a long summer stay doesn't come with a surprise bill.

Are all the sites really pull-through?

All eleven. You drive in and drive out, with room for a full-length coach plus a towed vehicle. If you're running something unusually long, call us and we'll confirm the fit before you book.

How far are you from OSU and Boone Pickens Stadium?

About five minutes. We're at 1919 N Jardot Rd on the edge of the Stillwater city limits — close enough that campus, the stadium and most restaurants in town are a short hop, far enough out that game-weekend traffic isn't parked outside your door.

Why do nightly and weekly rates change?

Stillwater fills up around the Oklahoma State calendar — football Saturdays, Homecoming, graduation and move-in weekends. Our standard rates are $60 a night and $250 a week; those dates are priced separately. Send us your dates and we'll quote them exactly.

What can I put down the sewer hookup?

Normal waste and enzyme- or bacteria-based tank treatments only. We run an aerobic septic system, which treats waste with live bacteria, so formaldehyde-based holding tank deodorizers, bleach and other harsh chemicals kill it and take the system down for the whole park. Check your tank treatment for the word "enzyme," "bio-active" or "septic safe" — and if you're unsure what you're carrying, ask us before you dump.

What time is check-in and check-out?

Check-in is 3 PM and check-out is 11 AM. If you're arriving after dark, give us a heads up and we'll have your site ready.

Are dogs allowed?

Absolutely — and they get their own fenced dog park on the property. We just ask that pets stay leashed elsewhere in the park and that you clean up after them.

Do you take monthly guests?

Yes — $500 a month, full hookups and WiFi included, and it's our best value. It's popular with travel nurses, contractors, students and snowbirds. With only eleven sites, monthly availability is worth asking about early.

What's the WiFi like?

Free and park-wide, included with every stay. No daily code, no per-device charge.

Before You Arrive

Arriving at the park

Please don't dump chemically treated waste

We're on an aerobic septic system — live bacteria do the treating. Formaldehyde-based holding tank deodorizers, bleach and other harsh chemicals kill those bacteria and take the whole system down, which is an expensive, smelly problem for everyone in the park.

Use enzyme- or bacteria-based tank treatments only. Look for "septic safe," "enzyme" or "bio-active" on the label, and avoid anything listing formaldehyde. If you're not sure what's in your tank, ask us before you dump — we'd much rather answer a question than fix a system.
